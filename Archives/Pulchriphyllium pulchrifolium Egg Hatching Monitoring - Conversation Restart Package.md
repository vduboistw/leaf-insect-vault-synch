## 7. Incubation and Hatch-Window Context

A species-specific breeder account for the Sukabumi culture reports:

- Incubation of about four months at 20-23 degrees C on slightly damp sand using the referenced breeder method.
- Approximately 60-70% RH described as sufficient.
- Eggs described as prone to mould, although the breeder reports that a thin mould layer did not reduce hatching ratio.
- An airy enclosure and substantial light recommended.
- Hatching reported after dawn, often around noon.

Source: [Pulchriphyllium pulchrifolium Sukabumi breeding notes](https://www.phasmatodea.com/pulchriphyllium-pulchrifolium-sukabumi)

These are breeder observations for a named culture, not controlled thresholds for Hannah's eggs. Her eggs spent most of their incubation in the warmer main-enclosure regime, commonly around 26-29 degrees C. No validated species-specific temperature-development model is available here, so warmer conditions must not be converted into a confident earlier hatch date.

Using the breeder's four-month figure only as a reference centre:

| June laying boundary | Four-month reference date |
|---|---|
| First confirmed eggs, 2026-06-09 | Around 2026-10-09 |
| End of cohort, 2026-06-29 | Around 2026-10-29 |

September is a plausible early-watch period because of the warmer history. October is the main reference window, while November and later remain plausible. Failure to hatch during October is not by itself evidence that the cohort has failed.

The project egg paper, *Leaves that walk and eggs that stick* (Buescher et al., 2023; DOI: [10.1186/s12862-023-02119-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-023-02119-9)), describes the water-responsive pinnae and adhesive secretion of many phylliid eggs. It provides useful morphological context but does not establish incubation thresholds or a hatch schedule for this *P. pulchrifolium* cohort.

## 9. What to Record

For each environmental or inspection update, record only what is available:

- Observation date and approximate time.
- Whether the record covers a full day, partial day or spot reading.
- Temperature and RH values, with graph-derived estimates marked as approximate.
- Visible condensation, standing water or egg-surface wetness.
- Vermiculite condition and any water added or removed.
- Ventilation or enclosure changes.
- Egg count and whether all 31 are accounted for.
- New mould, colour change, deformation, damage, operculum displacement or other structural change.
- Hatch evidence: nymph observed, opened operculum, empty shell or uncertain event.
- Hatchling posture, ability to climb, limb extension, initial activity and any entrapment concern.
- First confirmed feeding, first frass and first moult for each hatchling when observed.
- Every intervention and its reason.

Photographs should be interpreted conservatively. Describe visible features before proposing a biological explanation.

## 10. Hatchling Welfare Response

When a hatch occurs:

1. Record the time found and the evidence of hatching.
2. Observe posture, limb extension, grip and ability to climb before intervening.
3. Avoid unnecessary handling.
4. Ensure an easy climbing route is available from the egg cup.
5. Provide fresh, suitable young foliage promptly. Guava is the colony's confirmed primary food plant; wax apple is confirmed as accepted by this stock.
6. Keep the hatchling separate from the adult enclosure while it is very small.
7. Record feeding marks or frass as stronger evidence of successful feeding than proximity to a leaf.
8. If a nymph appears trapped or malformed, document the condition before deciding whether intervention is justified.

Do not diagnose a failed hatch, deformity cause or environmental cause from a single observation.
