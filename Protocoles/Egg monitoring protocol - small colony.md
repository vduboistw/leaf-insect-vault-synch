# Pulchriphyllium Egg-Laying Health & Safety Monitoring Protocol for a small colony (less than 10 adult females)

## Purpose

Use ongoing egg-production records primarily as a **health and safety monitor for actively laying females**.

The daily tally is not intended to become a permanent detailed ledger. Its main purpose is to detect changes in individual laying regularity, possible hidden or missed eggs, and patterns that could justify closer observation for retained eggs / egg-binding or other reproductive problems.

## 1. Rolling monitoring window

Maintain and display approximately the **latest 10–14 days** of egg-production information whenever a new laying update is provided.

Older daily detail does not need to remain in the rolling display once it falls outside this window, although monthly/cohort totals should be retained separately.

## 2. What to record for each update

For every new egg-production input, record when available:

- Date and approximate inspection/collection time
- Number of eggs collected
- Individual female attribution
- Confidence of attribution:
  - **Confirmed** — laying/extrusion/flick witnessed
  - **Supported/inferred** — strong evidence from location, timing, or established individual pattern
  - **Assumed** — useful working hypothesis but not securely attributable
  - **Unattributed** — insufficient evidence
- Egg size or morphology when noteworthy:
  - Regular / near-regular
  - Small
  - Very small
  - Other unusual morphology, pigmentation, crest development, fragility, etc.
- Relevant contextual observations such as food changes, environmental changes, hidden eggs, delayed collection, or caretaker changes

Do not force individual attribution when the evidence is insufficient.

## 3. Health-monitor interpretation

Evaluate each new update against the recent rolling history.

The primary question is **whether each actively laying female continues to show a reasonably regular laying pattern**, rather than whether the colony as a whole is producing an expected numerical quota.

Natural day-to-day variation is expected. **One day without an egg is not by itself an alarm.**

Pay particular attention when:

- One female appears to stop laying while another continues normally
- An individual's gap becomes conspicuous relative to her established rhythm
- Colony-level production remains normal but individual attribution is insufficient to demonstrate that all females are still laying
- Laying declines together with behavioural or physical changes
- Egg morphology changes substantially or persistently
- An unusually large collection may represent hidden/missed eggs rather than a genuine production burst

An unexplained individual interruption should trigger closer observation of that female and consideration of retained eggs / egg-binding, without diagnosing egg-binding from egg counts alone.

## 4. Colony-level versus individual-level conclusions

A healthy colony-level total **must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that every female is laying normally**.

Small-colony attribution rule: When the established laying rate is approximately 1.x eggs per female per day, finding at least as many fresh eggs as there are actively laying females within a period of less than 24 hours may be treated as supported evidence that each female has laid at least once during that interval, unless location, timing, individual laying patterns, or other observations suggest otherwise. This is an inferred attribution, not a confirmed one.

When recent eggs are unattributed:

- State that colony production is continuing
- Identify the date of the last individually confirmed or supported egg for each female
- Mark individual continuity as unresolved when appropriate
- Continue monitoring until attribution or subsequent observations clarify the situation

## 5. Egg quality and morphology

Egg quantity is the primary reproductive-safety monitor, but egg quality should be tracked when meaningful changes occur.

Relevant observations include:

- Progressive size reduction or recovery
- Very small eggs
- Reduced lateral crest development
- Unusual pigmentation
- Unexpected fragility
- Other structural abnormalities

Morphological changes should be interpreted alongside laying frequency, individual identity where known, food quality, environmental conditions, and female condition.

## 6. Hidden and delayed egg recovery

Eggs discovered after delayed cleaning, vacuum recovery, or in hidden enclosure locations should be included in the appropriate cohort total when possible, but **should not be assigned to a specific laying date or individual without evidence**.

They should not distort the rolling daily health monitor as though they had just been laid.

## 7. Standard response to each ongoing egg update

Each new egg-production update should normally include:

1. The new observation and updated current cohort/month total
2. The **rolling 10–14 day laying monitor**
3. Individual attribution and confidence where available
4. A **laying-female safety monitor** showing the last individually supported laying evidence for each active female (red/amber/green visual flagging)
5. A brief assessment of whether any individual regularity concern or egg-binding warning is present
6. Relevant egg-size/morphology trend when applicable

The health/safety interpretation takes priority over commentary about milestones or raw cumulative production.
